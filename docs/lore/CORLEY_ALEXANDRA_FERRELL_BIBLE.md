## 0. Authority & Reading Rules

This bible is the single authoritative statement of who the protagonist is. Where any other
BLACK VECTOR document contradicts it, **this bible wins for protagonist-specific narrative fact**.
Previously accepted *mechanics* are preserved unless an entry below explicitly supersedes a
protagonist-specific narrative fact.

Rules:

1. The protagonist is **female**. She is **Corley Alexandra Ferrell**.
2. **THE HAND** is her military identity — not a separate person, not a new person created on
   the island.
3. The protagonist is **one coherent human being** across soldier, mother, murderer, prisoner,
   experiment and survivor. The design must always let a reader separate:
   - what she **was**
   - what she **did**
   - what the **world believes**
   - what **she believes**
   - what is **actually known**
4. Do not flatten her into female action hero, generic trauma survivor, emotionless assassin,
   innocent victim, psychopath, or superhero.
5. Do not resolve D029/D030/D031 material here. Where a question belongs to those directives,
   it is marked `REQUIRES_FUTURE_DECISION`.

**Canon audit classification key:** UNCHANGED · REINTERPRETED · SUPERSEDED ·
REQUIRES_FUTURE_DECISION.

## 1. Canonical Identity

The protagonist is **Corley Alexandra Ferrell**. Military identity: **THE HAND**. She is female.

The Hand is *not* a new person created on the island. She is the ruined continuation of a woman
who already had an extraordinary life before BLACK VECTOR begins.

Her identity must contain **all** of these truths simultaneously:

- woman
- patriot
- renowned soldier
- elite overwatch reconnaissance operator
- legendary Hidden Hand operative
- team leader / protector
- mother
- wife
- daughter
- sister
- friend
- convicted murderer
- death-row prisoner
- Black Hand experimental subject
- survivor

These are not a checklist of plot roles. They are layered and simultaneous. She is not "a soldier"
in one scene and "a mother" in another; she is always all of it at once, and the game is the
pressure between them.

**Forbidden reductions:** female action hero · generic trauma survivor · emotionless assassin ·
innocent victim · psychopath · superhero.

> Canon check: "The Hand" as a *name the world uses* and "Corley" as *the woman* are the same
> person. INNER_CORLEY is not another entity; it is the part of her that still speaks when the
> armor is on.

## 2. Corley Before the Fall

Define Corley as a **complete person** before the tragedy. She had a full inner life, a sense of
humor, people she loved, and an identity that was not built from trauma.

Required traits — all of them:

- loving
- fiercely protective
- maternal
- emotionally perceptive
- patriotic
- highly disciplined
- dryly funny
- profane when appropriate
- capable of brutal military humor
- deeply loyal
- extraordinarily patient operationally
- precise rather than physically domineering

Her **command presence** is strong without being a screaming caricature. She does not dominate
through volume or threat. She dominates through attention, preparation and certainty. People
follow her because she is exceptional; they trust her because she cares whether they come home.

Her **humor** combines military shit-talk, impeccable timing, dry observation, protective
affection, and occasional brutal understatement.

The Hidden Hand understands the difference between:

- **Corley insulting someone because she loves them**, and
- **The Hand correcting an operational failure**.

Everyone who served with her learned to tell those apart instantly. That distinction is the
warmest expression of who she is, and the player should feel its absence when it is gone.

## 3. The Hidden Hand as Her Real Family

Corley's Hidden Hand unit is the most genuinely real social bond of her adult life.

They are:

- real people
- genuine friends
- genuine teammates
- genuine emotional family

Their assignments and larger institutional purpose may have been manipulated by Black Hand. **Their
relationships with one another were real.** The institution was false; the family was not.

Corley is the unit's **maternal center**. This is not because the others are incompetent. It is
because she:

- watches everyone
- remembers everyone
- anticipates problems
- protects younger operators
- knows when someone is afraid
- notices injuries
- maintains cohesion
- carries responsibility

They do not merely respect The Hand. **They love her.**

Frozen distinction:

> **They followed her because she was exceptional.**
> **They trusted her because she cared whether they came home.**

Composition (working, preserved): SECOND · RECON · POINT/BREACHER · HEAVY · TECH · MEDIC.
Corley is founder and commander. The unit is a genuine mixed-gender family, not a "brotherhood."
Some operators are women; sexes are not frozen per role. The term **"brotherhood" is superseded**
as the unit's defining label wherever it excludes or erases her.

## 4. Hidden Hand Synchronization — Personal Context Only

> D029 owns the full program. This section establishes only Corley's lived experience.

Her unit operated with extraordinary synchronization because of:

- elite shared training
- extensive operational history
- trust
- complementary specialization
- emotional bonding
- shared Compound exposure
- emerging neurological entrainment

From **inside** the unit, this does not feel like telepathy. It feels like knowing people so well
that speech becomes increasingly unnecessary — the kind of wordless coordination only possible
between people who have depended on each other for years.

Corley considers this normal for **them**. She does not initially understand its experimental
significance. She has no frame of reference telling her that a unit should not be this synchronized.

The player may notice the "how do they do that" quality before Corley does. It is not a power.
It is a bond the program was quietly measuring.

## 5. The Photograph

The surviving Hidden Hand photograph is a **major persistent character artifact**. It is not an
ordinary collectible and must never be treated as one.

**What it is:**

- one of the last physical proofs the unit existed
- evidence of a life increasingly erased from official history
- proof to Corley that there was once a version of herself people loved
- emotionally far more important than its material value

**When taken.** At the unit's operational peak, in the Hidden Hand era — a candid team photograph
taken on the ground, not an official portrait. It belongs to the period the prologue dramatizes
(OP HALF-LIGHT and the peak missions).

**Who is visible.** The complete original team — SECOND, RECON, POINT/BREACHER, HEAVY, TECH, MEDIC —
with Corley at the center. Individual teammate fates are NOT finalized here; D029 owns the broader
unit aftermath. The photograph records the team as whole, which is precisely why its loss and
fragmentation matter.

**Why Corley still possesses it.** It was the one meaningful personal possession retained through
arrest, trial, conviction and imprisonment. It was confiscated at least once and returned. It was
still on her at the point of covert diversion, and it survives into Black Hand custody. The
starting lock of the main game includes the photograph.

**Physical deterioration across prison / island history.** The print degrades progressively and
must be visually continuous with the living-save law:

- prison era — worn, repeatedly folded and unfolded, edges softened by handling
- death row — thin, creased, a corner fold that becomes a recurring motif
- Black Hand captivity — water damage, staining, deliberate rough handling
- island — blood-stained, torn, repaired with tape or stitching; possibly annotated

The **fold pattern and corner crease** are legitimate recurring visual/textual motifs (the
D018 §34 memory/echo budget references the photograph's fold-shape).

**How it participates in later recognition / reveal scenes.** The photograph is the emotional
key to the surviving-teammate arc: during the doctrine §25 confrontation it falls into view and
the former SECOND recognizes both the photograph and the woman beneath the prisoner. It is also
a candidate trigger object for the first undeniable anomalous event, so that the moment lands on
the player's emotional anchor rather than on a system object.

> Do not turn it into a collectible, a craft material, or a stat item. It is memory made physical.

## 6. Manufactured Civilian Life

Corley believed her civilian family life was natural. Later canon reveals Black Hand manipulation.

- Her **husband** was placed/staged as part of the wider program.
- Her **children** were intentionally part of an experimental reproductive/lineage program.

**But the emotional relationships were real to Corley.**

Forbidden framing:

> "None of it mattered because it was fake."

Correct framing:

> **Black Hand manipulated the circumstances around authentic human attachment.**

Corley genuinely:

- loved her husband
- loved her children
- believed she had built a life outside the military
- believed her children represented something that belonged to her rather than the program

The children later connect to the longer generational/island saga. **Do not fully resolve that
endgame mythology in D028.** Names of husband and children, the exact lineage-program mechanism,
and the generational endgame are all `REQUIRES_FUTURE_DECISION` (D029/D030 and later saga canon).

> The horror is not that she loved a lie. The horror is that she loved for real inside a design.

## 7. Compound Withdrawal and Collapse

Chronology is preserved. Corley does **not** know she has been receiving the Compound during
military service. After leaving the controlled supply chain, exposure stops or changes. Her
deterioration is fragmented and progressive.

Required symptoms / themes (may include):

- insomnia
- paranoia
- memory disruption
- emotional dysregulation
- rage
- sensory disturbances
- intrusive experiences
- deteriorating certainty

**Ordinary self-medication** — alcohol, cannabis, or similar — may appear as failed coping
behavior. It is **not** the root cause and must not be presented as the explanation for what
follows.

**Do not** use the Compound as a simplistic excuse that removes responsibility. The Compound
explains a mechanism, not a moral absolution. She still did what she did.

Preserved supporting canon: the program recognized warning signs before she understood them,
monitored them, and chose not to intervene (BLACK_HAND_PROVENANCE §13).

## 8. The Family Killings

The atrocity remains narratively **real and central**.

- Corley believes she killed her family.
- The public believes she killed her family.
- The legal system convicts her for it.

Later evidence may reveal manipulation, missing context, compromised memory, engineered
circumstances, or program involvement.

But D028 preserves this rule:

> **Corley cannot simply escape her guilt by discovering that nothing happened.**

The game may **complicate responsibility. It must not cheaply erase consequence.**

No clone, no other-actor substitution, no "it was all a fabrication" reveal. What varies is
context and degree of manipulation — never the fact that people she loved are dead and that she
was the instrument.

> This supersedes the older hedge "His family *may* be among the dead / exact sequence partially
> uncertain." The family are among the dead; she is convicted; the *exact sequence and its
> manipulation* remain partially uncertain and revisitable.

## 9. Public Monster / Private Woman

After the killings, the public mythology of Corley becomes radically different from who she was.

Public interpretation may include accusations such as:

- remorseless
- manipulative
- narcissistic
- sociopathic
- monstrous
- dangerous
- calculating

Treat such psychological labels as **in-world interpretations, not authoritative diagnosis.**

The tragedy is the gap:

| Layer | Question |
|---|---|
| WHO CORLEY WAS | the loving soldier, mother, friend |
| WHAT SHE DID / IS BELIEVED TO HAVE DONE | the family killings |
| WHO THE WORLD SAYS SHE IS | the monster |
| WHO SHE NOW THINKS SHE IS | the prisoner |

That contradiction is core BLACK VECTOR material. The game never flattens it into a single truth.

## 10. Prison Transformation

Prison does not simply make her stronger. **It teaches her to perform the monster everyone
already believes she is.**

Define the transformation:

- stops explaining herself
- stops expecting belief
- conceals emotion
- becomes increasingly detached
- becomes physically hardened
- becomes frighteningly procedural about violence
- develops survival behaviors incompatible with her previous social self

But underneath:

- grief remains
- maternal instincts remain
- guilt remains
- attachment capacity remains
- humor remains, buried
- fear remains
- love remains

She becomes colder externally while becoming more psychologically fragile internally. The armor
is not strength; it is load-bearing and cracking.

Repeated prison violence ultimately contributes to her death-row status (a NEW capital
conviction arising from later killings committed during incarceration — preserved chronology,
BV-D076). The prison era is where she learns to be dangerous *without* the discipline that once
made her safe to be around.

## 11. Death Row → Black Hand Recovery

Chronology preserved:

Corley expects execution. Her final understood destination is death. Instead she is covertly
diverted into Black Hand custody.

She undergoes:

- transport
- containment
- medical / experimental procedures
- fragmented consciousness
- uncertain passage of time

She does **not** initially know how long she has been there.

Then catastrophe / chaos breaks containment. She awakens in the **Simse Sound** environment
believing, at least initially, that she may actually have died. The island can plausibly feel
like punishment or hell.

**Do not resolve that uncertainty immediately.** The player should be able to entertain the
possibility that she is dead or in some afterlife of the program, even as the world behaves
with ruthless physical consistency.

Preserved: secret diversion mechanism remains fictional/opaque (BV-D076); she is stripped of
her old identity and kit at awakening (BV-D078).

## 12. Island Corley

The beginning-of-game Corley is **not** pre-fall Corley. She is:

- emotionally armored
- hypervigilant
- suspicious
- ruthless when necessary
- exhausted
- guilty
- highly competent in ways she may not consciously understand
- resistant to attachment
- deeply afraid of becoming emotionally responsible for another person

Yet remnants of The Hand continuously leak through:

- protecting people automatically
- battlefield analysis
- checking injuries
- situational awareness
- dry humor
- leadership instincts
- inability to kill a helpless person without cause

That contradiction drives reclamation. The player is watching a professional who swore she was
done caring discover that she still does.

## 13. Four Faces of Corley

Four distinct but continuous expressions of **one** person:

| Face | Definition |
|---|---|
| **CORLEY** | The private human being — the woman, not the function. |
| **THE HAND** | The disciplined military/operator identity: precise, watchful, decisive. |
| **INMATE FERRELL** | The prison-created armor: detached, frightening, transactional. |
| **INNER CORLEY** | The person the player hears but other characters often cannot see. |

**These are not multiple personalities.** They are contextual identities and defenses belonging
to one person. She moves between them the way anyone moves between a work voice, a mother voice,
and an alone-in-the-dark voice. The game should make the seams visible without implying
dissociation-as-disorder.

The dramatic engine: the world interacts with THE HAND or INMATE FERRELL; the player increasingly
longs for CORLEY; INNER CORLEY is where she still exists uncensored.

## 14. Voice Doctrine

Corley's **spoken** voice and **inner** voice must differ.

**Spoken Corley** (especially early island):

- concise
- controlled
- hard
- emotionally withholding
- dry
- profane selectively
- rarely explains herself

**Internal Corley** may reveal:

- fear
- tenderness
- guilt
- tactical cognition
- suppressed maternal instincts
- disgust with herself
- dark humor
- intrusive memories
- doubt

**NPCs do not hear internal Corley.** Her inner voice is player-only and never a mechanic an NPC
can react to. Channel isolation is absolute (see BV-D151 / the four-channel narrative law).

Do not make internal monologue constant. **Silence remains important.** The most powerful inner
line is often the one she does not say.

## 15. Humor Doctrine

BLACK VECTOR may contain brief, unexpected humor. Humor must arise naturally from:

- military culture
- character personality
- brutal timing
- logical absurdity
- dry observation
- Shade literalism later
- old-team familiarity

Forbidden:

- Marvel-style constant quips
- tension-breaking comedy routines
- self-aware genre jokes

Desired reaction:

> the player unexpectedly laughs once, shakes their head, then the darkness resumes.

Corley's returning humor is a subtle **indicator of psychological reclamation**. When she starts
making the old jokes again, she is coming back — and she may hate that it is happening.

## 16. Reclamation Arc

Corley's progression is **not** bad person → good person. It is **not** weak → powerful.

It is:

> **LEGEND → COLLAPSE → CONVICTED MONSTER → EMOTIONAL ARMOR → SURVIVOR → FORCED
> SELF-CONFRONTATION → RECONNECTION → CHOSEN IDENTITY**

Recovery is expressed through **behavior**, not cutscenes. Examples:

**Early:**

- orders
- uses people transactionally
- avoids attachment
- suppresses concern

**Later:**

- asks rather than commands
- uses names
- waits for responses
- chooses mercy
- accepts help
- allows humor to return
- accepts that caring makes her vulnerable

The progression is **not linear**. Regression is allowed and expected. A late-game scene may show
her acting exactly like the inmate again, and the player should feel the fall.

> Mechanical band mapping (preserved, D017 §3): LEGEND (prologue) → BROKEN SURVIVOR (main-game
> opening) → RELEARNING → RECOVERING OPERATOR → INTEGRATED HAND. The five mechanical reclamation
> stages remain valid. The arc above is the **narrative identity** overlay on those stages.

## 17. Shade Boundary

> D028 establishes only Corley's psychological predisposition. D029 owns full Shade canon.

Corley initially keeps the Shade because:

- he is dangerous
- leaving him creates future risk
- killing a helpless controlled young man in cold blood violates something still alive inside her
- she can rationalize keeping him as useful

She will initially treat him **more harshly and transactionally than her deeper motive warrants.**
The player may hear the contradiction through INNER_CORLEY: the spoken justification and the true
reason do not match.

Preserved: the Shade is a separate victim of the same machine — not a former teammate, not a
failed version of Corley, not someone who understands her abilities. Hand and Shade are mirrors,
not versions of each other. The canonical mercy choice stands.

The gender dynamic is now female protector / young male charge. This is a deliberate contrast to
her maternal history and is `REQUIRES_FUTURE_DECISION` only in its full dramatization (D029).

## 18. Surviving Hidden Hand Teammate Boundary

Preserve **one** major surviving former teammate / right-hand operator: the **SECOND**.

He must eventually function as:

- living evidence of Corley's prior life
- someone capable of hating her
- someone capable of recognizing The Hand beneath the prisoner
- someone whose own Black Hand damage differs radically from hers

His historical relationship with Corley must be strong enough that later hatred and
reconciliation have real weight: he was among those closest to The Hand — the person who trusted
her line most, and therefore the person most destroyed by believing she betrayed it.

Do not fully design his psychological/perceptual architecture here. **D029/D030 own that.**

Preserved mechanics: doctrine §25 scene lock (the three-shot confrontation; the photograph falls
into view; recognition); §26 the former SECOND becomes the primary persistent ally with real
limitations; BV-D062 the 1–2 survivor framework. His name and specific backstory remain
`REQUIRES_FUTURE_DECISION`.

---

## 19. Anomaly Boundary

Preserve:

- Corley had **no overt telekinetic powers during the Hidden Hand era**.
- Anomalous capability emerges later through long-term Compound exposure, withdrawal,
  subsequent experimentation, and neurological adaptation.

Do **not** redesign D016 here. D030 reconciles the anomaly with the revised protagonist narrative.

The unit's synchronization (Section 4) reads, in retrospect, as extreme intuition and
entrainment — never overt telekinesis. Pre-collapse feats remain deniable.

Anomaly remains a mirror of her inner state: it surfaces under strain, it costs her, and it is
never the source of her identity. **The Hand's value was always her judgment, not her power.**
