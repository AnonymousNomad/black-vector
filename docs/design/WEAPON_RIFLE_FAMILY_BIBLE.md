## 1. Design philosophy

1. **BLACK VECTOR firearms remain recognizable firearms.** Even advanced weapons generally evolve through: better
   sensors, better integration, networking, optics, materials, ergonomics, stabilization, information systems —
   rather than lasers, plasma rifles, unlimited smart ammunition, or magic sci-fi ballistics.
2. **Universal rule: every advantage creates a liability.** Nothing in this ecosystem is free; anything that gives
   an edge imposes a cost, a failure state, or a dependency (frozen model, §21).
3. **Weapons are equipment that belongs to someone.** Civilians, frontier users, security, conventional military,
   contractors, Hidden Hand, Black Hand, Shades, experimental programs each keep their own weapon cultures (§13–§16).
   A weapon family is authored once, then derived across factions/ages/conditions/configurations (§3).
4. **THE frightening part is the INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE, not the cartridge.** The Shade rifle line (§6 Family 11,
   §27) is the strongest expression of this rule: dangerous because of what it knows and shares, not because it
   fires something absurd.
5. **The impossible stays uncommon and provenance-gated.** Experimental descendants are rare, unstable, labeled,
   narratively justified, and costly (BV-D028/CHEMICAL-ceiling rules; §28).
6. **The Hand's edge is skill, not equipment.** Technology multiplies what he can do; it does not create him (§12).
7. **No rarity-color loot distribution.** A weapon exists where someone plausibly carried, stored, hunted with,
   guarded with, tested, or abandoned it (§23).

## 4. Core long-gun families (frozen)

Card format: ROLE / PROVENANCE / USER / HANDLING IDENTITY / ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY / ADVANTAGE / COST /
FAILURE & MAINTENANCE / VISUAL IDENTITY / FLAVOR DNA (= underrepresented-real language, §5 only as art/history).

### Family 1 — COMPACT CQB CARBINE (working institutional code **R-1**)

- ROLE: point/breacher and interior work — facilities, ships, tunnels, containment, vehicle-secured operations
  (MILITARY_ROLES role 2; D011 §4 Point/Breacher).
- PROVENANCE: institutional entry-weapon lineage, standard security inventory.
- USER: Black Hand security/armed response, QRF, some facility contractors.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: short, hot, immediate — fast transitions and tight-corner authority; reduced reach and
  stability relative to larger platforms.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: indoor-first; moderate outdoors; salt-sensitive if carried exposed on the outer coast.
- ADVANTAGE: maneuverability, rapid handling, low-bulk carry in confined spaces and vehicles.
- COST: compressed engagement envelope; shorter reach and stability; precision forgives nothing past a gate.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: compact mechanisms clear poorly once mud/crud accumulates; parts scarce on island;
  institutional standardization makes the family easy to keep in service where a log exists.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: short profile, minimal furniture overhang, muted institutional steel; reads "entry".
- FLAVOR DNA: compact Cold War interior patterns; an optional bullpup arrangement as a faction variant.

### Family 2 — GENERAL-PURPOSE MODULAR CARBINE (**R-2**)

- ROLE: the ordinary military workhorse; wide role flexibility; "many tanks, one platform" storytelling.
- PROVENANCE: conventional military service standard; over time extends into security and contractors (D011 §26
  SECURITY / CONVENTIONAL MILITARY / CONTRACTOR possession).
- USER: security, contractors, some military, some Black Hand personnel.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: balanced, neutral, predictable; carries substantial visual variation (lengths, optics,
  furniture swaps) without becoming a new weapon every time.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: most forgiving of the rifle families across snow, mud, salt — a mature pattern.
- ADVANTAGE: flexibility, familiarity at scale, parts-standardization; the easiest rifle on the island to keep alive.
- COST: ordinary — no specialist edge; heavier than the compact; a common-family report the whole island hears.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: few surprises; wear is slow and visible; standardized parts make field repair reality.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: modular rail architecture, standard-issue optics, desaturated institutional finish — reads
  "service standard".
- FLAVOR DNA: modularity; a roller-delayed-style action flavor drawn from European leaf (fictional, never a copy).

### Family 3 — RECON / LOW-SIGNATURE CARBINE (**R-3**)

- ROLE: reconnaissance, stealth patrol, wilderness observation, infiltration (roles 3/7).
- PROVENANCE: recon/observation lineage, short action, quiet-heritage line; the scout-rifle walkability concept.
- USER: recon/stealth operators, wilderness patrol, compartment scouts.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: quiet, patient, light — an all-day-carry rifle that rewards patience over volume.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: cold-weather friendly; fine components are the FIRST casualty of salt and moisture.
- ADVANTAGE: low bulk, mobility, subdued equipment profile, sensor compatibility.
- COST: shorter reach; reduced raw stopping pressure; exotic quiet-kept parts that an island log cannot supply.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: quieting and fine-tuning degrade first; maintenance is specialist, not garrison.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: slim, tapered, understated, soft muted fabrics — reads "scout/recon"; no flashy trim.
- FLAVOR DNA: scout-rifle weight/walkability; Nordic/Eastern cold-weather survey line (art only).

### Family 4 — BATTLE RIFLE (**R-4**)

- ROLE: exposed terrain, experienced operators, harder targets, longer engagements (QRF/heavy, role 8).
- PROVENANCE: full-power tradition; older institutional stock that survives in good hands as legacy issue.
- USER: experienced operators, QRF/heavy, garrison legacy holdings, some Black Hand.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: heavy, weighty, deliberate — recoil character dominates the read; slow to shoulder.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: exposed and windy terrain forgives its mass; dry storage is its friend.
- ADVANTAGE: harder-hitting, longer reach, barrier-leaning behavior.
- COST: weight, recoil abstraction, slower handling, ammunition burden (heavy loads strain the island carry).
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: ammunition stores bite logistics; recoil-driven wear on older examples; parts available
  only where the institution left a cache.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: long steel and legacy furnishing — era-layered, "old but loved" institutional remnants.
- FLAVOR DNA: pre-1960 full-power tradition; Eastern European infantry legacy (art only).

### Family 5 — DESIGNATED MARKSMAN PLATFORM (**R-5**)

- ROLE: bridge between carbine and precision rifle; mid-range refined shooting (role 3).
- PROVENANCE: marksman/observer upgrade lineage over service patterns.
- USER: marksman/overwatch operators, Black Hand technical staff, some garrison.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: calm, delivery-driven, sits between the battle rifle's mass and true precision's patience.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: optics-first — cold, fog, and rain test the glass more than the action.
- ADVANTAGE: extends the operator's reach before true sniper equipment; **the family that can re-wake The Hand's
  old instincts on the way back** (D013 §4; §24 reclamation).
- COST: not a true long-range weapon; not a close-fight rifle; mid-weight; zero and glass discipline required.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: zero defeats happen in cold/weather; fine adjustments bleed under heavy field use.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: refined furniture, adjustable cheek architecture, larger glass, modest institutional distinction.
- FLAVOR DNA: Central European optics-first line; a Czech/European polyline read (art only).

### Family 6 — MOBILE PRECISION RIFLE (**R-6** lineage; The Hand's natural family)

- ROLE: mobility, observation, precision, concealment, field carry, overwatch — The Hand's signature family.
- PROVENANCE: precision/overwatch lineage; Black Hand holds a standardized R-6 pattern for sniper/overwatch roles;
  the Hidden Hand compartment carried low-volume fitted iterations of an earlier generation of the same family
  (institutional absorption, D012 lineage reflex).
- USER: The Hand (historic compartment iteration), sniper/overwatch operators, boss-contract scale threats.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: patient, weighty-but-walkable; build, geometry, and patience — the rifle that rewards a
  firing position.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: built for field carry and overwatch; exposed cold observation for long shifts.
- ADVANTAGE: precision at range while staying portable and concealable — the overwatch family.
- COST: demands setup discipline; no close-fight character; heavier than carbines; optics-dependent identity.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: fine geometry is fragile to drops and salt; a lifetime zero the island cannot re-certify.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: long, disciplined silhouette, minimal drag, concealment-first color, fitted personalization on
  the compartment iteration — reads THE HAND before any exposition (BV-SKILL-022 §41.3).
- FLAVOR DNA: northern "walking rifle" tradition; Nordic cold-weather precision patience (art only).

### Family 7 — HEAVY PRECISION PLATFORM (**R-7**)

- ROLE: barrier/severe engagement where the firing position is part of the mission (boss contracts, doctrine §23).
- PROVENANCE: institutional heavy-barrier inventory; rare on the island by design.
- USER: rare heavy-support operators; QRF/heavy; boss-contract threat scale.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: mass, setup burden, limited mobility, reduced CQB usefulness — unmistakably not a walker's gun.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: heavy steel loves stable cold air; hates the island's mud and salt, and portage.
- ADVANTAGE: extraordinary reach and barrier behavior for the moment it is needed.
- COST: mass, setup time, ammunition/logistics burden, and a near-universal CQB penalty.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: the platform is dependable but its LOGISTICS crater on an island; losing or damaging it is
  a mission statement, not a minor setback.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: big action, heavy barrel architecture, institutional tripod/support language.
- **It must never become the universal best answer** — its cost stack is the entire point (D013 §4; doctrine §9).

### Family 8 — PDW / COMPACT DEFENSIVE PLATFORM (**P-1**)

- ROLE: technical staff, pilots, vehicle personnel, medical/security staff, close protection — small enough to
  exist naturally through facilities without every employee carrying a full rifle.
- PROVENANCE: compact defensive inventory; sits beside the sidearm in the ecosystem (§6).
- USER: non-combat specialists, drivers, guards of convenience, close-protection operators.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: small, quick, defensive — present and ready, never a fighting carbine.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: ideal indoors/vehicles; marginal in open terrain.
- ADVANTAGE: always-present defensibility, low bulk, natural facility carry.
- COST: short range, reduced stopping edge, no assault-rifle authority.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: often the least-maintained class in garrison (carried daily, serviced rarely); compact
  mechanism needs patient clearing.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: machine-pistol degree of compactness, institutional pouch carry — reads "staff/facility".
- FLAVOR DNA: compact Cold War defensive machine-pistol degree; Central European (art only).

### Family 9 — COMBAT SHOTGUN (**G-1**)

- ROLE: security, containment, breaching abstraction, frontier ownership, wildlife defense.
- PROVENANCE: two substantially different variants: institutional (containment/breach, standardized) vs civilian
  frontier (hunting/wildlife, personal). Different organizations use different guns.
- USER: security/containment teams; hunters, homesteaders, guides, locals.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: close-fight authority with a heavy, cycling rhythm; brutal and honest at shotgun ranges.
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: excellent in tight wet interiors; frontier pattern survives salt and hard years.
- ADVANTAGE: overwhelming close pressure, breaching neutrality, genuine wildlife/defense tool.
- COST: short envelope, magazine/loading lag, ammunition bulk, recoil weight.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: action crud in mud/snow; stored shells age badly; frontier examples show deep honest wear.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: institutional = short polished steel and rails; frontier = wood and metal, worn stock, hunting
  trim.
- FLAVOR DNA: frontier workhorse plus institutional breaching language (art only).

### Family 10 — CIVILIAN / FRONTIER RIFLE (**no code**)

- ROLE: hunters, isolated families, guides, locals, scavengers — Alaska authenticity (D011 §15/§16).
- PROVENANCE: legacy single long-guns; family DNA without institutional standardization; local parts culture.
- USER: civilian frontier population, scavengers, homesteaders.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: old, damaged, familiar. **Old does not mean useless: a battered rifle in capable hands stays
  dangerous.**
- ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY: shaped by salt/wet exposure, remoteness, long storage, and scarce parts.
- ADVANTAGE: everywhere; socially invisible (a frontier rifle merely belongs); a last-resort survival object.
- COST: age, wear, atypical parts, and the worst condition curve of any class.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: worn mechanisms, taped repairs, mismatch shoulder slings; frequently at DAMAGED/CRITICAL
  on the wear ladder (§19).
- VISUAL IDENTITY: stock wear, tape, maritime salt, personal history — never showroom, never worthless starter loot
  (§16).
- FLAVOR DNA: older frontier hunting traditions; maritime work lines (art only).

### Family 11 — SHADE NETWORK RIFLE (**S-line**, [PURE BLACK VECTOR FICTION])

- ROLE: primary Shade firearm family; assault-rifle priority defines the Shade silhouette more than the blade
  (D011 §6; doctrine §41.4).
- PROVENANCE: G7-generation integrated small-arms line derived conceptually from the same service families, wrapped
  in T2 network architecture (BV-D050). **EXPLICITLY FICTION: no real integrated rifle of this level exists**
  (ledger family 11; BV-SKILL-025 invariant 6).
- USER: Shades (network infantry/recon/assault/containment/technical/controller), never civilians.
- HANDLING IDENTITY: calm, coordinated, network-rhythm — reads as an ordinary rifle until the architecture acts.
- **FUNDAMENTAL RULE: the frightening part is the information architecture around the rifle, not an absurdly
  powerful cartridge. No supernatural targeting. No legitimate observation = no target knowledge (§27).**
- VARIANTS: **S-1** entry carbine (Shade baseline) · **S-2** strike · **S-3** recon/sniper (overwatch role) ·
  **S-4** command rifle (longer action, controller/commander's rifle, D011 §6/§8).
- SENSOR/ARCHITECTURE: visor integration, shared target identification, network status, sensor fusion, squad data,
  commander information — all bounded by D011 §7 network truth (source+channel+timestamp; sector/latency/
  degraded-comms/jamming/controller-loss/desync/fallback).
- ADVANTAGE: information multiplication and coordinated battlespace — several weapons acting as one informed net.
- COST: network dependence (jamming degrades it), power sensitivity, exotic modules, not field-serviceable by
  locals, catastrophic loss cost.
- FAILURE & MAINTENANCE: desync isolates the Shade into the BROKEN/DESYNCHRONIZED family (D011 §6); module failures
  in cold; a desynced rifle is a quiet rifle, not a magic rifle.
- VISUAL IDENTITY: integrated optics/sensor/display architecture, restrained functional illumination, networked
  seams — the T2 expression of the Shade's contained-human identity (BV-SKILL-022).
- FLAVOR DNA: none real — PURE BLACK VECTOR FICTION at the rear edge of the gun line (BV-SKILL-022 tech ladder).---

## 6. Sidearm boundary

The pistol direction is substantially established (doctrine §4; BV-SKILL-011 starting lock). **This directive does
NOT rebuild handgun doctrine.** It only fixes how sidearms fit the ecosystem:

- **Role placement**: sidearms belong to close-protection, technical/vehicle staff, operators' transition until a
  rifle is recovered, and the frontier's old worn pistols (D011 civilians). The boot knife remains the premier close
  tool (BV-SKILL-011); the sidearm is a flanking fight, not a gunfight.
- **Identity**: preserve the preference for visually distinctive sidearms, less-overrepresented designs, strong
  character ownership. No Glock/M9/1911 defaulting; no new handgun families frozen here.
- **The Hand's hierarchy** places a **compact sidearm third**, after precision rifle and DMR/recon (§7) — his hand
  falls to it only after distance tools are exhausted.
- **PDW link**: the P-1 sits in the same ecological niche as the sidearm (natural facility carry) but is a distinct
  platform; one never replaces the other in design intent.

## 7. THE HAND — weapon identity

The Hand historically was: Hidden Hand **founder, commander, sniper, overwatch specialist,
reconnaissance-assassin, battlefield protector** (doctrine §14/§15/§41.3). His equipment must reflect that; he is
**never primarily an assault-rifle operator merely because assault rifles are common.**

Frozen hierarchy (descending):

1. **MOBILE PRECISION RIFLE** — his natural family (Family 6): mobility, observation, precision, concealment,
   field carry, overwatch.
2. **DMR / recon rifle** — the bridge that reconnects old instincts before proper sniper equipment returns (Family 5).
3. **COMPACT SIDEARM** — transition and close-defensive role only.
4. **KNIFE / THROWING TOOLS** — close manipulation, quiet work (BV-SKILL-009; §18).
5. **IMPROVISED WEAPONS when required** — the survivor's inheritance, never his identity.

The family that "strongly resembles the type of weapon The Hand historically preferred" is the player's eventual
precision recovery (§24). His loadout reads as discipline — patience, geometry, load — not as gear lust
(BV-SKILL-025 invariant 7; MILITARY_ROLES §8 guard).

## 9. Remote precision support platform

DESIGN at game-system level only. It supports these concepts:

- PRE-MISSION PLACEMENT (a firing position chosen before the engagement begins).
- BOUNDED FIRING ARC (fixed or narrowly contested geometry, never free fire).
- LIMITED AMMUNITION (a defined magazine/reserve; not an endless lane).
- SENSOR/VISOR CONNECTION (the Hand sees through its sighting arithmetic in the fused picture).
- DIRECT REMOTE COMMAND (human-issued targeting discipline — it does not decide).
- OPTIONAL CONSTRAINED AUTONOMOUS ASSISTANCE (bounded protocol, requires confirmation for escalation; telegraphed,
  counterable).
- TEAM-PROTECTION ROLE (a second lane protecting the same lane The Hand holds, or the team's route).
- RECOVERY AFTER MISSION WHERE POSSIBLE (it is equipment, not a disposable gadget).

**Hard limits (frozen):** setup time · weight/logistics · restricted traverse · line-of-sight limitations ·
communication dependence · jamming vulnerability · ammunition limits · power requirements · risk of loss.

Benefit/Cost/Failure/Dependency entry in §21. NO real automated-weapon construction instructions anywhere
(BV-SKILL-025 invariant 6).

## 11. Visor / forearm integrated picture

The Hand's helmet/visor, comm link, forearm tactical diagnostic interface, drone, remote rifle, and teammate
telemetry share **one fused tactical picture** (BLACK VECTOR does not fire-and-forget tech; everything reads as a

- **VISOR** — immediate combat-relevant information (verified targets, squads, net status, warnings) rendered so it

**Filtration is the capability.** The technology is powerful not because it floods he owns: **it filters information
effectively.** HUD overload is a designed anti-pattern, not a feature (BV-SKILL-022 restrained-illumination rule;
doctrine §41.2).

## 12. WHY THE HAND IS SPECIAL

Freeze explicitly: **equipment never explains his reputation.**

A different trained operator on the same system should NOT automatically perform like The Hand. His superiority comes
from: attention management · pattern recognition · marksmanship · leadership · anticipation · spatial understanding ·
decision speed · experience.

Technology MULTIPLIES those qualities. It does not create them. This is the load-bearing support for the prologue
fantasy: "one man appears to operate like several people simultaneously" — and it directly satisfies BV-SKILL-025
invariant 7 and MILITARY_ROLES §8 guard: the advantage is skill + perception applied to real geometry, never an
augmented-vision crutch.

## 13. Hidden Hand equipment philosophy

Hidden Hand gear reads: **low-volume · mission-specific · highly integrated · individualized by specialty ·
advanced but recognizable · maintained meticulously · difficult to replace.**

- NOT every member carries identical equipment; every operator eventually possesses a recognizable **specialty
  package** (overwatch, breaching, technical, recon…).
- Gear is compartment-cast: no inventory code, fitted iterations of familiar families, operator names.
- **Do NOT design the full original team's kit yet** — the prologue needs The Hand's signature package, and the
  second-in-command's state at the reunion (doctrine §25/§27) stays provisional. Roster/loadout detail is a D014
  concern (deferred).

## 14. Black Hand weapon culture

Black Hand guns communicate: **institutional standardization · containment · logistics · interoperability ·
clinical design · easy replacement.** The same detail across a line, uniform finish, rails and mounts for
institution-mounted accessories, log-numbered inventory.

**Contrast, frozen:** Black Hand standardization vs Hidden Hand personalization — identical at the family level,
opposite at the personality level (BV-SKILL-022 §41.9 faction separation).

## 15. Contractor weapon culture

Contractors carry: **mixed manufacturers/design traditions · commercial equipment · personal preference · uneven
wear · different generations of hardware.** A security contractor's clean-issue carbine vs a mercenary's
used-and-proven commercial platform side by side in one squad.

This yields visual variety without lore-breaking prototypes — the mix is the signature (commercial, pragmatic,
less standardized; doctrine §41.9).

## 16. Local / civilian weapon culture

Simse Sound residents possess: hunting rifles · shotguns · older sidearms · work tools · bows · utility knives.

Equipment reflects: **age · maintenance · salt/wet exposure · remoteness · parts availability · personal history.**
Condition climbs the wear ladder fastest here (§19). **Civilian equipment is never worthless starter loot** — a
frontier rifle in a capable hand remains dangerous (Family 10; doctrine §30 hunting/survival objects, still never
balanced as a combat arsenal).

## 17. Bows / silent traditional weapons

Bows may exist through: hunting · survival · scavenging · specialized stealth use. Multiple populations may hold

## 22. Handling axes (minimal frozen set)

Weapons are never reduced to damage/DPS. Freeze the **minimal useful set** (game-facing, NO numbers here; data
house is BV-SKILL-011):

1. **HANDLING** — draw/ready/transition readiness; how the weapon answers the wielder.
2. **STABILITY** — aim-hold and recovery; the calm the weapon allows.
3. **MOBILITY BURDEN** — weight/size penalty on movement and carry.
4. **EFFECTIVE ENGAGEMENT ENVELOPE** — the range/context band the weapon owns.
5. **SIGNATURE** — report/visual/thermal visibility of each shot (BV-SKILL-007 channel).
6. **AMMUNITION BURDEN** — weight, capacity, scarcity pressure on logistics.
7. **RECOVERY** — recoil settle and reload tempo.
8. **RELIABILITY** — condition-sensitive; the option to fail in the field.
9. **SENSOR INTEGRATION** — optics/network level the platform carries (T0–T2).
10. **ENVIRONMENTAL SUITABILITY** — Alaska serviceability read (§20).

Family cards in §4 are the design read; these axes are the game contract. Numerical balance is deferred to
BV-SKILL-011 when implementation is authorized.

## 27. Shade sensor weapon integration

Shade rifles consume only **legitimate network information** (D011 §7 truth, frozen): direct sight · teammate
sight · drone observations · fixed sensors · commander updates.

  a wall hack.
- Every network fact carries source + channel + timestamp; sector/latency/degraded-comms/jamming/controller-loss/
  desync/fallback bounds apply to what the rifle can know (§4 Family 11; D011 §7).

Experimental weapons stay RARE. Prefer: advanced sensor systems · stabilization · networking · unusual materials ·

(visible as a one-off, not a new line). G8/generation-gated, ability-contract-tied (D011 §26 EXPERIMENTAL
