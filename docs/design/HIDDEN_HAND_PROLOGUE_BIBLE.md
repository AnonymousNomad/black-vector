## 1. Opening emotional structure

Emotional spine, frozen: **ICON → BROTHER → FRACTURED VETERAN → KILLER → PRISONER → SUBJECT → SURVIVOR.**

| Episode (sequence) | Emotional beat | What the player feels |
|--------------------|----------------|-----------------------|
| OP HALF-LIGHT (peak mission) | ICON + BROTHER | awe of competence; warmth of the team; "this is the legend" |
| Homecoming + decline | FRACTURED VETERAN | unease; "something is wrong with him" |
| Public bloodscene | KILLER | horror + shame + responsibility (not empowerment) |
| Family catastrophe | KILLER (depth) | grief, guilt, disorientation, terrible certainty |
| Police / trial / prison / death row | PRISONER | compression, isolation, losing himself, injustice-as-process |
| Covert diversion + experimentation | SUBJECT | helplessness, violation, fragmented terror |
| Simse Sound awakening | SURVIVOR | bare survival, confusion, the start of reclamation |

The six required purposes map onto the spine (deliverable checklist):

1. **Prove through gameplay why Hidden Hand became legendary** → §3/§9 (peak mission, crisis earn).
2. **Prove why The Hand was respected and loved, not merely feared** → §10/§12 (team feel, extraction beat).
3. **Give a controlled taste of peak conventional gameplay** → §4–§7 (Layered Overwatch loop at 60–70%, §29).
4. **Destroy that identity through a fragmented fall from grace** → §14–§18 (decline, bloodscene, catastrophe).
5. **Establish that The Hand genuinely committed the later killings** → §16/§17 (canon, non-exculpatory).
6. **Deliver the player onto Simse Sound broken, confused, stripped of nearly everything** → §26 (awakening).

Pacing across the spine (§32) and per-sequence player agency (§31) are fixed below.

**The player begins believing they are playing a grounded elite military/espionage game.**

- NO opening exposition about Black Hand, the Compound, telekinesis, Shades, or the Darkness. None of those words,
  none of those images, none of those concepts precertified.
- The first mission makes The Hand appear **almost supernatural** through: skill · preparation · awareness ·

- Every "impossible" read in the opening must later survive scrutiny as credible fieldcraft (BV-SKILL-025

- If a player says "how did he do that?" the answer may remain open in-fection, but it is never answered here and

## 3. Prologue mission — PEAK HIDDEN HAND

The first fully playable sequence is a Hidden Hand operation at the unit's peak.

- **The complete original team is present** (as archetypal placeholders; full identities remain unfrozen, §10).
  Composition (working): SECOND · RECON · POINT/BREACHER · HEAVY · TECH · MEDIC. The unit is a genuine
  brotherhood/sisterhood (some operators may be women; sexes are not frozen per role).
- **The player controls The Hand as**: founder · commander · sniper · overwatch specialist ·
  reconnaissance-assassin · battlefield protector (doctrine §14/§15; D011 §8; D013 §7).
- **His principal value is not raw marksmanship. It is: "He sees the whole engagement."**
- The team trusts him because **threats routinely disappear before the others realize they were exposed** — a
  design law of the level, not a remark.
- The mission reads legitimate to the unit: a compartmented national-security tasking (working op name
  **OP HALF-LIGHT**; perceptual-family codeword per D012 Appendix A naming rules — mundane, image-neutral,
  decodable-only-later). The player is NOT shown the corruption behind the mission (doctrine §17: mission purpose
  deception is later knowledge).
- Site: an exposed coastal/relay facility at night in cold weather — an abstracted northern site, **named
  nothing and tied to nothing** (not Simse Sound, not any canon facility). Generic industrial/containment
  architecture learned from the world foundation's reference language ONLY ([ARCH] flavor, no real site).
- Technical credo for the level: **the mission proves the doctrine by demands, not by scripted kills** (§8/§9).

## 4. Layered Overwatch doctrine (loop and contracts)

Uses D013 §8–§11 frozen architecture. The Hand controls three information/action layers.

**PRIMARY LAYER — THE HAND.** His own position, precision rifle, observation, decision-making, marksmanship,
leadership. Remains the primary gameplay layer; the other two layers serve it.

**SECONDARY LAYER — REMOTE PRECISION SUPPORT PLATFORM.** Mission-specific portable overwatch system providing a
**bounded second firing lane**. NOT a generic sentry turret. Player actions (frozen):

- position it during setup (pre-mission placement);
- assign a watched sector;
- hold fire;
- designate a verified target (only targets the net legitimately holds — §5, D011 §7);
- invoke constrained support (bounded protocol; escalation requires confirmation; telegraphed, counterable);
- temporarily view its feed.

Hard limits from D013 §9 stand: setup time · weight/logistics · restricted traverse · line-of-sight ·
communication dependence · jamming vulnerability · ammunition limits · power · risk of loss. NO real
automated-weapon construction procedures; this is fictional game equipment only.

**SENSOR LAYER — OVERWATCH DRONE.** Multiplies information: team position · teammate vitals/status · visual
observation · verified target marking · route observation · remote-platform awareness · terrain/range context.
**No omniscience** (D011 §7/BV-D060; BV-SKILL-008). The drone does not discover anything the net did not
legitimately observe.

**GAMEPLAY LOOP** (the sobriquet that makes the peak feel earned):

```
OBSERVE  (fused picture: teammates, routes, arcs, the breach)
→ PRIORITIZE (attention is finite — §6)
→ INTERVENE (primary lane | remote lane | call-out that moves the team)
→ RECOVER (reposition, refresh sensors, rebalance attention)
```

The loop repeats under rising simultaneous demand until the crisis (§9) resolves and extraction (§12) closes.
Earned legend = repeatedly choosing the correct intervention under limited attention (§6, §8, §9).

## 5. Fused information system

The Hand's peak kit functions as **one integrated battlefield nervous system** (D013 §11).

**HELMET / VISOR** — displays only immediately relevant combat information. Candidate categories (frozen as the
complete set; no HUD creep):

- teammate identity
- teammate location
- simplified physiological status
- injury alert
- communications status
- verified threat marker
- remote-platform arc/status
- drone status
- extraction/objective information

**FOREARM TACTICAL DIAGNOSTIC INTERFACE** — deeper inspection/control:

- expanded teammate diagnostics
- drone tasking
- remote-platform status
- mission updates
- equipment state
- route information

**Design law: contextual surfacing over menus.** Information appears because it matters now, in the player's
current focus; nothing demands menu navigation mid-fight. Overload is a designed anti-pattern (D013 §11; BV-SKILL-022
restrained illumination). **The system filters effectively — that is its power** (§6).---

## 6. Human attention limit

**The technology does not make The Hand superhuman.**

- His reputation comes partly from being exceptionally capable of managing several information streams without
  being overwhelmed — a human excellence, not a cybernetic one (doctrine §8 §36.1; BV-SKILL-025 invariant 7).
- **Frozen principle: more active systems = greater cognitive management burden.**
- The player cannot flawlessly micromanage simultaneously: rifle · drone · team · second firing lane · objectives ·
  environment. Prioritization is forced; the peak moment is earned when the player prioritizes well under the load.
- **Conceptual bridge to Neural Strain (frozen):** the player learns, in the prologue, a bounded
  attention-management muscle — juggling active systems, reading cost, rebalancing focus. In the main game,
  Neural Strain (BV-D036) reuses that learned muscle by adding perceptual/motor overload ON TOP of the same
  attention stack, rather than introducing an unfamiliar second economy. No mechanical carryover — the prologue is
  not a tutorial for the Strain system — but the perceived pressure is continuous.

## 7. Team telemetry

The Hand monitors teammates through **legitimate tactical systems**: pulse/state · injury warning · position ·
communications · mission role · immediate distress.

- **Not a medical simulator.** Telemetry serves two purposes only: tactical (know where each operator is, whether
  they can move/cover/help) and **emotional**.
- **The player can literally see when a teammate is in trouble.** That visible distress is what makes the protective
  identity land (§10): The Hand watches their vitals the way a protector watches his people.
- Information is bounded and sourced (BV-SKILL-008): a remote status is a report, not a wall of truth — if comms
  drop, a teammate out of net is a "last known + silence" state, not an answered certainty.
- Crying/teammate distress must produce OPTIONS (call-out, lane shift, reposition, escalate support) — never just
  watching a bar drain while doing nothing (agency, §31).

## 8. Mission design

**Design ONE opening operation that proves the doctrine — no generic target practice.** The mission creates
simultaneous competing demands (frozen demands list):

1. team infiltration below,
2. live overwatch threats,
3. movement outside the expected plan (a route the brief did not predict),
4. one compromised route,
5. teammate exposure,
6. extraction timing,
7. incomplete information (the player is acting on partial, sourced knowledge — never a full tactical map of truth).

The Hand must **repeatedly choose what matters most**. His legend emerges because he keeps making the correct
intervention — he never "fixes everything," he repeatedly fixes the thing that matters, in time, at range, before
anyone else could (doctrine §29, §11 public legend).

Design rules:
- Every system the player touches (platform arc, drone, comms, telemetry, primary lane) must have a moment where it
  is the single right answer AND a moment where over-relying on it is the wrong answer (Benefit/Cost, §30).
- The level layout must present the "competing demands" as positions in space, not checkboxes in prose: distances,
  angles, cover, LOS, weather-light, time.
- Provide at least one earned, unforced optional intervention (a +operation that the crisis never requires) so the
  player experiences initiative, not only response — this is the "handle on the legend."
- NO cinematics that solve the mission for the player. Reputation earns (§9).

## 9. "HOW DOES HE DO IT?" moment

A mission crisis where several things go wrong **nearly simultaneously** (frozen requirement):

- one route is compromised (a patrol/noise exposure the team cannot see yet),
- a teammate becomes exposed and is drifting into the wrong sightline,
- a second lane threatens the breach from an arc the platform cannot cover,
- the remote platform reaches its arc/power edge at the worst moment,
- extraction timing is already compressed.

The player must: **identify the true priority threat · protect one teammate · cover another lane · adapt the remote
platform · maintain awareness of the broader mission.**

- **NOT a cinematic auto-win.** The player performs enough of it that the reputation feels earned. If the player
  fails to prioritize, the cost must be real and visible (a teammate hurt, a route lost, extraction rusked) — while
  always allowing the crisis to rebalance, not hard-fail the run.
- Achievement of the ideal sequence makes teammates **appear almost impossible to defeat while The Hand is watching
  them** — because threats vanish before they are exposed. That appearance is the fantasy this directive is built
  to deliver (§3).

## 10. HIDDEN HAND team feel

The opening establishes the unit as a **genuine brotherhood/sisterhood** (doctrine §15 brotherhood canon).

The members:
- know one another's habits,
- joke during appropriate downtime,
- trust one another,
- rely on The Hand personally,
- respect his judgment,
- do **not** treat him like an authoritarian commander.

He founded the unit, but **they follow him because he repeatedly brings them home.**

Scene language that must appear somewhere in the opening: shared call-sign shorthand; a dry joke at downtime; a
private reassurance between operators; spontaneous physical trust (cover-shoulder checks, handing a weapon, walking
into his line blind because they trust it).

**Archetypal placeholders: do NOT write the full roster.** Operator identities, faces, exact histories remain
unfrozen (D011 §14, BV-D062 framework; roster detail is future canon). The ONE member fixed by identity-role is the
**SECOND** (the canonical second-in-command / right-hand man of doctrine §25/§26 — the future primary ally and the
shooter of the three-shot scene). Everyone else is a functional placeholder with a firmly drawn temperament where the
scene needs one.

## 12. Prologue extraction

**The mission ends quietly, not with another explosive climax.** Frozen sequence:

1. The Hand secures or recalls his remote support system;
2. recalls/retrieves the drone;
3. conducts final overwatch;
4. reaches extraction **ahead of the rest of the team**.

**When the others arrive, he is already there.** Preferred character beat: he is smoking a cigar and casually
watching the perimeter. A teammate reacts with affectionate disbelief: **"How the hell do you do that every time?"**
(Exact dialogue is NOT frozen — the beat is.)

The scene includes: handshakes · fist bumps · shoulder contact · exhausted humor · relief · genuine affection.
Then the team boards extraction together.

**This scene must establish: "These people love this man."** It is the emotional keystone the fall-from-grace later
shatters (§13–§18). Without this beat, the fall has nothing to break.

## 14. HOMECOMING

First show **enough normalcy to establish he returned home as**: a respected veteran · a public military icon ·
a family member · a husband/partner where applicable · a functioning human being.

- A few quiet, warm, unmistakably normal beats: home, table, a familiar voice, routine. Short and real, not
  saccharine.
- Then fragmentation begins incrementally. **Do not make him immediately monstrous** — the decline must read as
  sickness first, danger second, so the eventual violence terrifies rather than validates.
- His observable state (tremor, short fuse, staring at nothing) is presented diegetically — the player sees him
  losing composure the way Control bands are described in doctrine §36 (COMPOSED → FRAGILE → BLEEDING) before a
  coherent "rage" ever manifests.

## 15. Fragmented decline

After returning home, chronology becomes unreliable. Use SHORT fragments (frozen palette — any subset, in bad-time
any order):

- insomnia; staring awake at night
- missing time; gaps in a day he "knows" he lived
- arguments without remembered beginnings
- unanswered teammate calls (the phone rings; he cannot pick it up; later, no one calls)
- isolation; retreating from people who love him
- shaking hands
- brief visual/audio disturbances (an important rule: these stay within the bounds of plausible stress/neurology
  here, NOT conspicuous paranormality — the answer to "was something else happening?" stays open, §27)
- nightmares
- waking somewhere unexpectedly
- alcohol
- occasional cannabis use as attempted self-medication
- increasingly severe rage
- memory gaps

**Frozen law: alcohol/cannabis are NOT framed as the cause of the violence.** The underlying cause is the fictional
neurological/conditioning collapse associated with long-term Compound history and program abuse — a truth the player
does not yet know (D012 §13; §28 here). Self-medication is presented as a failed coping reaction, never a moral
explanation, never "the drug did it."

## 16. Public bloodscene

Eventually The Hand regains awareness in a **public location amid blood/chaos**. He cannot immediately reconstruct
what happened.

- **Frozen canon: over time the game makes clear enough that he genuinely committed violence.** This is
  responsibility, not ambiguity-dancing (doctrine §18; D012 §14).
- **NOT a sandbox civilian-killing sequence.** Presentation is controlled/semi-playable: fragmented perception,
  aftermath, incomplete memory — the goal is **horror and responsibility, not empowerment** (BV-SKILL-018 ambiguity
  discipline; doctrine §3 memory as peril).
- The player should feel the un-victorious ache of having done something with "him" at the center — never the kick
  of doing it virtuously.
- Authoring rule: this sequence may show body/chaos truthfully but must not instruct, glamorize, or linger on the
  mechanics of harm (no actionable content; the player never "plays the kill" as gameplay skill).---

## 17. Family catastrophe

**Preserve current canon: The Hand subsequently kills members of his own immediate family during the catastrophic
collapse. This event is REAL.**

Forbidden later reveals (frozen, hard): a clone did it · Black Hand physically performed the killings instead ·
the family was imaginary · the entire event was fabricated. Later revelations provide **context, not exoneration**
(doctrine §18; D012 §14; Pillar RESPONSIBILITY §34).

**Because of subject matter, present through** (frozen presentation stack):

- fragmented perception,
- controlled interaction,
- aftermath,
- incomplete sensory memory —

**NOT freeform domestic massacre gameplay.** No control granted to perform the act upon living civilians as a game
skill; no camera delight in the act. The player must understand **what occurred without the game celebrating it**
(BV-SKILL-018: horror/scarcity/ambiguity; doctrine §23: never routine enemy content, and the descent here is story,
not content loop).

Emotional intent: this is the moment "KILLER" ceases to be a threat-read and becomes a grief-read — the icon, the
brother, and the broken veteran all die here. It is the deepest cut of the fall, and it must land before prison so
that everything afterward carries it (grief-colored authority in the rest of the through-line).

Authoring law carried from D012 §13: do not conflate mental illness with violence; the violence is specific to this
fictional conditioning/withdrawal history and individual circumstance. No stigma statement is authored; only this
man's torn history.

## 18. Police / tactical response

After the domestic catastrophe:

- barricade/standoff,
- law-enforcement response,
- escalating tactical containment,
- eventual tactical police/SWAT intervention,
- capture.

**The Hand is dangerous enough that the response feels credible** — the police who take him are not lambs; the

Player agency here is deliberately thin: limited movement, rising containment, the decision to stand down or be
overwhelmed — enacted as LIMITED PLAYABLE / INTERACTIVE CINEMATIC (§31). The capture is not a stealth-puzzle or a
combat win; it is the end of his freedom and the beginning of the silent institutional machinery (§19–§22).

## 19. Sentencing & prison (legal-thread logic)

For legal plausibility, frozen:

1. **The initial conviction may result in life imprisonment** (the strongest, most survivable reading of the
   existing "sentenced toward death row" canon — doctrine §18).
2. He spends **years incarcerated.**
3. During imprisonment: Compound absence persists · neurological instability remains · rage/control are severely
   degraded · prison survival behavior replaces military composure · he becomes increasingly feared · violence
   continues · procedural memories remain beneath the surface (doctrine §35.4 early-game Hand identity; framework
   band BROKEN/PRISONER §38.1).
4. **If he later reaches death row, a NEW capital conviction arises from later killings committed during
   incarceration** — not an administrative transfer from life imprisonment (this is the anti-incoherence rule of
   D014 §39 stop conditions; protects legal-logic sanity).

- **Do not overbuild courtroom simulation.** Trial/courtroom is swift, procedural, mostly off-screen or fragment;
  the life-killing gravity is conveyed by the sentence landing like a door.
- The player encounters prison as experience (survival, fear, cruelty, moments of lost dignity), not courtroom
  theater.

## 22. Covert diversion

Near the apparent endpoint, **continuity breaks again.**

- The player believes he is headed toward execution.
- Then: **discontinuity.** Black Hand secretly diverts him into the Simse Sound program.
- **Do NOT proceduralize how a real prison transfer could be illegally executed; the mechanism remains
  fictional/opaque** (doctrine §19 guard; D012 §14 "fictional covert mechanism"; the player learns it as a bare
  fact of wake-up, never as a how-to).

The diversion is shown obliquely: a transfer that isn't a transfer, corridors that aren't a courtroom, a door that
doesn't open where it should. The player recognizes the discontinuity before the explanation — exactly the
knowledge-boundary rhythm this bible freezes (§28; §13 hard tonal cut returns).

## 26. Main-game awakening (Simse Sound)

§15 convergence; ALASKA_WORLD_FOUNDATION §3 S-1, §12-J first slice).

The Hand awakens amid:

- shoreline/weather
- wreckage
- blood
- dead/injured guards or personnel
- emergency lights
- alarms
- damaged containment infrastructure
- abandoned equipment
- evidence of sudden catastrophe

He possesses almost none of his old identity or kit. **Absent:** Hidden Hand team · precision rifle · drone ·
remote platform · integrated visor · operational command structure.

**He has:** damaged body · fragmented memory · prison instincts · buried training · rage · pain · survival need.

**THAT contrast is the foundation of the reclamation game** (doctrine §3 memory progression; §32 disciplines;
D013 §24 reclamation-through-weapons; D008 framework bands: he starts at BROKEN/PRISONER and climbs toward THE
HAND). The opening's promise (§29) is that something worth reclaiming exists, and the player just watched it.

## 27. Prologue power boundary

- **Do not show mature telekinesis in the Hidden Hand mission.**
- **Do not establish paranormal capability as obvious fact anywhere in the opening.**
- Any unusual pre-collapse battlefield awareness must remain explainable as: training · intuition · perception ·

## 32. Pacing (recommended structure — approximate)

Recommendation is a structure and proportional targets, NOT hard timestamps. Goal: long enough to enjoy peak Hand,
care about Hidden Hand, and understand his reputation — short enough that Simse Sound still feels like the game's
BEGINNING, not Act II.

Approximate proportionality of the entire opening (boot → S-1 full control):

| Beat | Share | Read |
|------|-------|------|
| OP HALF-LIGHT (peak mission) | ~35–40% | the crown jewel; earned legend |
| Homecoming + decline | ~10–15% | falling, fast |
| Public bloodscene + catastrophe | ~8–12% | horror + responsibility |
| Police + capture | ~4–6% | compression |
| Prison → death row | ~10–15% | selected fragments + montage; the door closing |
| Diversion + experimentation + nurse + catastrophe flash | ~8–12% | fragmentation corridor |
| S-1 awakening (transitional) | ~4–6% | the hand-off into the real beginning |

- Total opening target (approximate): **~60–110 minutes** depending on player skill and optional interventions
  in the peak mission. Substantively less loses the brotherhood; substantively more undermines "the game begins on
  the island."
- Simse Sound (S-1 → S-3 wedge) is presented and felt as the START of the game world, i.e., the true thesis of
  reclamation. The opening is the fallout of the world; the island is the world.
- Ship gates (progress checkpoints) belong to the fall's bigger beats (departure from peak → home arrival → first
  heavy fragment → public bloodscene → catastrophe → court → diversion → awakening), never to mission internal
  seconds.
