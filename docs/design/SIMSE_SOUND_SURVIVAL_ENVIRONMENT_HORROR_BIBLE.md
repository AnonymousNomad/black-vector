## 2. Purpose — the systemic identity of Simse Sound

Simse Sound must function as MORE than a sequence of combat levels. It is simultaneously (frozen):

```
environment          — the island itself is legible, hostile, weather-driven
survival problem     — cold/wetness/exposure/shelter are decisions with cost
community            — people live here in viable clusters with needs and knowledge
infrastructure network — power, heat, water, communications, access, transport are systems
predator ecosystem   — wildlife is credible and informative, never a monster shooter
military disaster    — the program's collapse left ruins, materiel, and survivors
research site        — the program seeded plants, labs, records, and subjects
psychological weapon — the island attacks certainty as much as it attacks health
mystery              — the impossible exists here, sparse and unclassified
```

Governing world rule (frozen, verbatim):

> **Simse Sound must already be dangerous before anything supernatural occurs. The impossible makes an already
> hostile place harder to understand.**

This is the anti-coastline trap: weather, cold, terrain, wildlife, and combat must ALL be credible dangers on their
own, so that when the impossible surfaces it reads as addition, not replacement.

## 3. The four overlapping islands (frozen taxonomy)

The same geography carries four simultaneous interpretations. Any location can be (and usually is) any of these at
once; the player assembles meaning by walking through all four.

| Interpretation | Content | Function |
|---|---|---|
| NATURAL ISLAND | maritime-subarctic weather; coastline; cliffs; snow; rain; freezing wind; forests; waterways; unstable terrain; tides; wildlife; darkness; isolation | Nature can injure or kill the player with NO assistance from any faction |
| HUMAN ISLAND | homes; old communities; docks; fishing infrastructure; cannery; maintenance buildings; service roads; power systems; workshops; boats; radio infrastructure; shelters; abandoned civilian spaces | The world that existed before the present catastrophe; restorable and inhabited (§22–§24) |
| PROGRAM ISLAND | Black Hand research; containment spaces; covert logistics; medical areas; restricted infrastructure; surveillance; sealed levels; experimental wards; military/security installations; buried historical layers | The thing that happened here; layered and compartmented (D011 §5/§7) |
| IMPOSSIBLE ISLAND | events that cannot be comfortably classified as environmental, psychological, technological, historical, or anomalous | Sparse by law (§29–§33); never replaces the other three islands |

Composition rule: a single asset/place is authored across the interpretations it serves (e.g., the cannery is HUMAN
island + PROGRAM island in its sealed cold-storage; the mine is NATURAL + HUMAN + PROGRAM in its deep levels; the
Annex is PROGRAM first and HUMAN only in its ruin).

## 4. Core world interaction law (frozen)

"Do not make 'interactive environment' mean every loose object receives an interaction prompt."

Prioritize CONSEQUENTIAL INTERACTION. A useful interaction changes one or more of:

```
survival · access · information · population behavior · infrastructure · environmental safety ·
faction pressure · future routes · resource availability · horror state
```

- Interaction is predominantly SYSTEMIC rather than decorative: the world changes state, and that state propagates
  (§21). A restorable switch, a rerouted circuit, a cleared access are state changes; a movable stool is not.
- Interaction classes are BOUNDED (§27). Ambient decoration is visually distinct from interaction surfaces
  (BV-SKILL-006 expectation doctrine). If a player reasonably expects a surface to matter, it matters; otherwise it
  is background and reads as background.
- Every consequential interaction is OBSERVABLE: it has a state, a reason, and a logged transition (BV-SKILL-015;
  SOP-006). This keeps the systemic world debuggable and the horror-fairness law intact (§30).

## 6. Survival philosophy (frozen)

> **BLACK VECTOR survival is decision pressure, not meter maintenance.**

- NO constant eating/drinking chore loop. Hunger/hydration meters remain DROPPED (D004 §8). Fresh water is abundant
  by setting; the pressure is place-based — "where can you get clean water and warm, dry shelter HERE?"
- The primary pressures are: **cold · wetness · exposure · injury · exertion · shelter · weather · navigation ·
  equipment condition · occasional sleep/fatigue pressure**.
- Philosophy test (frozen): for every survival crisis, at least two genuinely different answers must exist (travel vs
  shelter; burn fuel vs conserve; push on vs recover; improvise vs seek support). If one answer strictly dominates,
  the system is not a decision — it is a chore (§30 stop condition).
- Survival must speak the SAME data as stealth and graphics: cold/wetness are authored truth consumed by signature
  emission, visibility, and weather alike (BV-SKILL-017; D004 §9). One truth, many consumers.

## 8. Secondary statuses (as conditions, NOT meters)

Evaluate the following primarily as CONDITIONS that feed the main model — short-lived, contextual, consequence-bearing
states with a trigger and an exit — not additional permanent HUD bars:

```
wet · soaked · bleeding · pain · chilled · hypothermic risk · exhausted · sleep-deprived ·
sick · concussed · encumbered · contaminated · equipment failure
```

- Conditions ATTACH to a host (CONDITION / CORE TEMPERATURE / EXERTION / locomotion) and multiply its effects. Example:
  BLEEDING attaches to CONDITION and drains it until treated; CONCUSSED distorts vision/balance and blocks precise
  marksmanship reads; ENCUMBERED subtracts from EXERTION and adds signature noise; CONTAMINATED (program residue) is
  rare, provenance-gated, and ties to treatment/search rather than a chem-meter.
- The HUD stays compact: conditions surface as contextual icons/edges or on the diagnostic screen, never as a wall of
  bars (§45). Sleep is a "few pressure decisions per multi-hour stretch", not a timer chore.

## 9. Cold — Simse Sound's signature pressure (frozen bands)

Cold affects actual play through: reduced exertion recovery · slower fine-motor actions · poorer weapon handling ·
Control instability · slower traversal · increased injury risk · greater shelter dependency. This is NOT a medical
simulator. Freeze game-facing BANDS (numeric curves pending device validation; the BANDS are the design contract):

| Band | Reads as | Gameplay effect |
|---|---|---|
| NORMAL | warm enough | full capability |
| COLD | first bite | slightly slower fine-motor; active recovery reduced; faint shiver present |
| CHILLED | significant | handling degraded, traversal slowed, tremor begins, Control instability risk in combat (D008 reading); breath/audio cues |
| HYPOTHERMIC RISK | dangerous | movement seriously reduced, weapon handling damaged, Control degradation, injury risk up, shelter dependency strong; vision/audio cues |
| SEVERE | crisis | collapse risk; the player must be warmed/rescued; near-incapacitation; can kill |

- Cold is COMMUNICATED diegetically — animation, audio (breath, wind, fabric), hand tremor, movement signature, HUD band,
  environmental cues — NEVER through constant numerical inspection.
- Cold couples to STEALTH: shivering raises movement/audio signature (D004 §9; BV-SKILL-007 channel model). Being cold
  is a tactical problem, and shelter/heat has signature consequences (warmth = light/smoke/sound).
- Cold couples to CQC: at CHILLED+ the Hand's Control reading and fine combat timing degrade in a legible banded way
  (consumes D008 interfaces; no separate combat subsystem authored here).

## 10. Wetness (frozen as a multiplier/condition)

- Wetness STRONGLY ACCELERATES cold danger. Sources: rain · snow melt · ocean spray · river/shoreline exposure ·
  immersion · damaged shelter.
- Model position (frozen): wetness is a CONDITION (dry → damp → wet → soaked) that multiplies the Core Temperature
  drain rate and increases thermal band speed; it dries via time + shelter + heat/fire and via clothing decisions.
- It is NOT a large standalone HUD bar. Represent as a contextual condition icon/edge + diegetic cues (weight, sheen,
  audio), with the steady-state readout on the diagnostic screen. The model lives in the shared weather truth —
  rain/snow state directly drives wetness input (one truth, many consumers).
- Decision meaning: clothing choice, shelter timing, and route selection are wetness decisions; getting wet in a storm
  is a real tactical event, not a background animation.

## 11. Clothing & protection (situational tradeoffs, frozen)

Clothing matters through: insulation · weather resistance · mobility · weight · drying · noise · durability.

- NO apparel spreadsheet gameplay. Items are defined as LAYERS (base/wet-shedding layer/insulating layer/outer shell +
  footwear/headgear/gloves) with a small bounded value set: warmth, weather resistance, mobility tax, weight, noise
  profile, durability/drying. Each layer is a real, countable, carried item — the collection is curated, not a loot
  pool of +stats jackets.
- Gear choice produces SITUATIONAL tradeoffs: an insulated parka warms you but is loud, heavy, slow to dry, and slows
  fine motor; a waxed shell dries fast and is quiet but offers little insulation wet; the boots that keep feet dry
  reduce creek-crossing speed.
- Clothing is ALSO a stealth surface (signature volume) and a survival surface (weather resistance) — it participates
  in the shared truth. Progress/finding better cold gear is a survival-recovery arc, not a stat grind.
- Equipment failure (§8): damaged/worn layers lose performance; repair is a TOOL-class interaction (§27) at shelter
  WORKSHOP capacity or via the ally (BV-SKILL-020).

## 12. Shelter (frozen levels)

Shelter is strategically meaningful and exists on a LEVELED scale:

| Level | Example | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| EXPOSED | open ground | baseline heat loss, no wind protection, no save/rest |
| WINDBREAK | rock overhang, wreck wall | reduces wind chill; limited drying; temporary rest |
| CRUDE SHELTER | tarp/lean-to, cave mouth | reduces exposure; basic drying; improvised rest |
| INTACT UNHEATED STRUCTURE | standing building without power | heat recovery slow; drying; sleep; crafting/repair possible with tools |
| HEATED STRUCTURE | powered/restored facility or fire | strong recovery; full rest; workshop/medical; this is a home-anchor (§24) |
| SECURED REST LOCATION | a claimed facility core (BV-SKILL-020) | full save/rest and capability recovery; the narrative home base |

- Shelter affects: temperature recovery, drying, sleep, crafting/repair, save/rest opportunities, and VISIBILITY TO
  ENEMIES (a lit window, smoke, heat signature — darkness/light, §20).
- DO NOT make every interior automatically safe. An unsecured building can be a patrol route, an animal den, a
  program casualty shelter, or the site of a horror event. Safety must be earned (SECURE stage of the RESTORE
  pipeline) and verified, which is itself information.

## 13. Injury & status architecture (frozen)

- Injury is a CONSEQUENCE of the world (fall, attack, cold exposure, animal encounter, industrial hazard), never a
  periodic tax (doctrine §30; BV-SKILL-017).
- CONDITION is composed from a small contextual BODY-STATUS model: HEAD / TORSO / LEFT-AND-RIGHT ARM / LEFT-AND-RIGHT
  LEG, each in NORMAL / INJURED / BADLY INJURED states, plus CONDITION-level trauma. This is a body DIAGRAM on the
  diagnostic screen, not a six-bar HUD.
- Each wound maps to a legible consequence: a leg wound slows locomotion and raises signature; an arm wound degrades
  marksmanship/fine-motor; a torso wound drains CONDITION and risks worsening; a head wound risks CONCUSSION and
  perceptual instability (bridging, carefully, into BV-SKILL-018 — NEVER used to randomize the player's world rules,
  §30 fairness).
- Treatment is DECISION-RICH: improvise (splint/bandage/field dressing — risky, slow), shelter-and-rest (time,
  resources), seek the ally's medical support at the restored base (BV-SKILL-020), or the rare intact medical
  equipment at a facility (§24) — each with cost in time, resources, and exposure.
- Injuries never heal-instantly-at-a-checkpoint; recovery consumes time that the world uses (storms arrive, patrols
  move, seasons/weather change, resources deplete). Injury is a pacing tool and a truth-teller about choice.
- Vitals must be HONEST: if the fiction ever PERCEIVED-modifies what the player sees of their own body (horror beat),
  the WORLD-truth body state is intact and the overlay separates the layers (BV-SKILL-015). Death never comes from an
  unknowable fake rule (§30).# — CONTINUED: PART II — WORLD SYSTEMS —

## 15. Storms as island-scale events (frozen + CANDIDATE content)

- Major storms occasionally act as island-scale EVENTS (not random punishment). They can alter: routes, visibility,

- They create TACTICAL OPPORTUNITIES as well as danger: sound masking for infiltration, discontinued patrols, exposed

- Scheduling law (frozen): storm timing is authored/semi-authored (season ladder + seeded event slots), observable,

## 17. Infrastructure consequence law (frozen — extends D013's four-term law to the world)

Restoring something must NOT automatically equal pure improvement. Every restore carries a full burden:

```
BENEFIT            — what the player/community gains
COST               — fuel, maintenance, signature, attention, resources
FAILURE STATE      — what it does when it breaks (outage, fire, damaged distribution)
DEPENDENCY         — what it needs to keep running (generator, fuel, grid path, maintenance)
SECONDARY CONSEQUENCE — what else the operating system now attracts (lights/thermal/radio/machinery reveal
                       occupation, may awaken connected systems, may put the holder on Black Hand's map)
```

Canonical example (frozen shape):

```
Facility power:
  Benefit        — heat, light, equipment, medical, tools
  Cost           — fuel, maintenance, signature (light/heat/sound/radio)
  Failure State  — outage, fire, damaged distribution, equipment damage
  Dependency     — generator + fuel + distribution path (grid/circuit)
  Secondary      — restored lights/heat radio-reveal the facility to patrols/Shade/net; draws scavengers;
                   powers OTHER restored systems that then carry their own consequences
```

This law applies to EVERY restoreable system in §16 and anchors doctrine §31's "RESTORE POWER/HEAT/ACCESS" stages.
The world does not quietly improve; every benefit purchases risk.

## 18. Electrical abstraction (frozen)

Because facilities depend heavily on infrastructure, define a game-facing electrical abstraction:

```
source  ->  distribution  ->  local circuit  ->  device group
(gen/power plant) (substation/bus) (floor/room feed) (lights, heat, medical, tools, comms…)
```

- Player-facing tasks stay UNDERSTANDABLE: restore source · reroute · isolate failure · choose priority load. No
  professional electrical-work simulation, no real hazardous-work instruction. FICTIONALIZED game abstraction only —
  the world models "which loads can run if I give this circuit power", not phase/amp math.
- Failure/degradation is a state the player can read (POWER_STATE at circuit/device-group granularity, observable via
  SOP-006), and repair choices carry the §17 law (a reroute that feeds the workshop starves the medbay).
- This is the LINE BETWEEN the world's systems and the interaction classes (§27 POWER/HEAT/COMMUNICATION/…).

## 19. Power priority as design material (frozen decision shape)

Interesting decisions may include choosing between: heating · lighting · medical systems · security · communications ·
workshop · elevator · surveillance.

- Rule (frozen): not every facility can necessarily support every load simultaneously. A restored facility with a
  small generator may power heat OR medbay OR workshop, not all three. The player chooses the LOAD SCHEDULE in-fiction
  (through the system, not a dialogue menu — world-as-interface).
- Consequences flow from the §17 law: heating a home draws fuel and signature; running an elevator signal-and-power
  may attract attention; powering a relay extends the community's reach AND its exposure (communications are not a
  one-way gift, §23).
- Priority choices are REVERSIBLE but always COSTED (fuel, time, rework, failure risk). They are a decision engine,
  not a puzzle.

## 20. Darkness, light & fire (frozen)

- Lighting affects: navigation · safety · stealth · human morale · detection · horror.
- Restoring lights makes navigation easier while COMPROMISING concealment (signature). Portable light is the same:

- Fire/heat sources provide warmth · drying · visibility · morale, while creating smoke · light signature · fuel

- Power restoration (§18/§19) drives darkness/light state across a district; a facility that "comes alive with light"

## 21. World-state propagation model (frozen — bounded, event-driven)

Actions at one site sometimes affect another. Examples at DESIGN level (frozen list — the bounded scope):

| Action | Propagation effect |
|---|---|
| Communications restored | new information elsewhere (and new exposure — §23) |
| Weather station restored | storm forecasting becomes available (and can feed patrol behavior) |
| Power rerouted | one facility gains while another loses (load scheduling, §19) |
| Tunnel opened | new route AND new threat access (logic connection, not teleport) |
| Medical equipment restored | survivor/community outcomes improve (community state §23) |
| Radio restored | factions can find the player (it is a beacon, not only a receiver) |
| Boat repaired | islands/sectors become accessible (transport is a network edge) |

RULES (frozen):

1. **Avoid an enormous global simulation.** The propagation model is BOUNDED: a small set of world-state nodes
   (facilities §24, communities §22–§23, infrastructure networks §16–§19) with EXPLICIT edges. Each edge carries:
   trigger condition · output delta · latency · cost. Nothing propagates except through a declared edge.
2. Event-driven, not per-frame: world-state changes when a player/system action completes a trigger; the world does
   not simmer continuously.
3. Off-screen behavior is COARSE (§28): a facility not near the player holds a state, not a simulation.
4. Every propagation is OBSERVABLE (BV-SKILL-015/SOP-006): state-value + reason + edge-id on the world overlay.
5. Propagation must NEVER create omniscience (D011 §7; BV-SKILL-008): information gained at site A reaches site B
   only through the declared communications edge with latency and degradation, exactly like the Shade network model.
6. Factions react to propagated signs only through their OWN bounded perception (BV-SKILL-008), never via the
   propagation graph telepathically.

Architecture recommendation (frozen shape): a WORLD-STATE GRAPH data layer (nodes: facilities/communities/networks;
edges: declared propagation rules with cost+delay) owned as DATA/resources, consumed by sector activation

## 29. Horror principle (frozen)

> BLACK VECTOR horror attacks **certainty**, not merely health.

The player repeatedly questions:

```
what happened · when it happened · whether a memory is theirs · whether something was physical ·
whether a person is alive · whether the environment actually changed · whether Black Hand really understands
the phenomenon · whether The Hand himself is reliable
```

Every horror structure must land on at least one of the narrative pillars (IDENTITY / BROTHERHOOD / RESPONSIBILITY,
doctrine §34; BV-SKILL-018). Horror is never noise. And it is always FAIR (§30).

## 30. Fairness law (frozen — verbatim)

> **The game may make the player doubt reality. It must not make the player doubt whether the game itself works.**

Locked rules:

```
- input remains trustworthy
- saving remains trustworthy
- critical HUD state remains functionally trustworthy
- bugs must never be intentionally mimicked
- death cannot arbitrarily occur from unknowable fake rules
```

Design corollaries: mystery remains DESIGNED, never frustrating. The overlay (BV-SKILL-015) is always truthful about
WORLD vs PERCEIVED even when the character is being deceived; a perceptual event is an authored, greppable fact with
a reason, never ambient chaos (BV-SKILL-018 invariant 4). The player's diegetic confusion is designed; the player's
trust in the CONTROL is never violated. If the fiction makes the player hallucinate a door, the door is PERCEIVED-
layer and the WORLD layer and controls still obey honest rules — the player can still walk, aim, read true tools;
only the content differs. Gameplay rules themselves never silently flip.

## 31. Memory Bleed system (frozen taxonomy)

Reconcile D014 Appendix B into the live canon. Four escalating categories:

| Bleed | Duration | Player experience | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| MICRO BLEED | seconds | present moment intact; a fragment intrudes | voice, face, gunshot, family fragment, prison sound, old teammate, visual replacement of a detail |
| WALK-THROUGH BLEED | minutes | player keeps movement/control while the present environment TRANSFORMS | hospital → home; facility → prison; mine → old mission. No loading-screen language where technically feasible |
| INTERACTIVE BLEED | event-length | the player can walk, interact, speak, fight, choose INSIDE the event | a full re-lived fragment where choices matter within memory |
| REALITY BREACH | extremely rare | something from the bleed possesses physical consequence AFTERWARD | an object, a wound, new information, a changed environment, another witness |

- Soak/dry rule (frozen): bleeds are authored events with IDs, scarcity budget, and a memory-truth layer (§32);
  they occur as the CONDITIONING/MEMORY substrate (D012), not as ambient rolling dice. Isolation/darkness/strain
  (BV-SKILL-018; doctrine §22) condition WHEN they surface — tied to the survival/weather cadence, never random.
- REALITY BREACH must be EXTREMELY rare and carry consequence-clean design (BV-SKILL-018 invariant 7): even at its
  most ambiguous, the breach produces causal, remembered world effect where the fiction allows — or the player is left
  wondering with AUTHORED evidence (a clue to resolve later), never a shrug.
- Do NOT derive all bleeds from one mechanism (paranormal vs conditioning vs memory vs anomalous) — D015 leaves the
  mosaic deliberately open (matches D011 §19 MANIFESTATION/ECHO/TRUE UNKNOWN sub-typing).

## 32. Memory truth levels (frozen)

Preserve REALITY / MEMORY / UNKNOWN.

- The player should SOMETIMES know which layer they experienced; SOMETIMES not. This is CONTEXTUAL and DIEGETIC (bible
  D014 §28 knowledge boundaries feed this) — NEVER a giant UI label stamping every event.
- When the player CAN determine truth, the evidence is authored and legible (a corpse that is real vs a stated memory;
  a note that is real vs a hallucinated scribble). When they cannot, the AMBIGUITY is the content (doctrine §23–§24).
- Consistency with BV-SKILL-015: WORLD/PERCEIVED split is ALWAYS technically true in the overlay even when the fiction
  is ambiguous to the CHARACTER. The tools never lie; the world may.

## 33. Horror frequency budget (frozen)

Psychological events lose power when constant. Freeze a BUDGET (qualitative; numeric targets authored later per
slice under BV-SKILL-016/018):

- Most traversal permits: **silence · ordinary environmental tension · human danger · anticipation.**
- Anomalous/paranormal-class moments are SCARCE and authored (D011 §30 rarity classes; doctrine §23 "never routine").
  Saturation law holds: the ordinary layer (weather, wildlife, locals, survival) stays visibly present at all times —
  it is the GROUND that makes later tiers read (D011 §3).
- Budget accounting: per-slice and per-sector counts of PARANORMAL-class events are ledgered (BV-SKILL-018 threat-class
  ledger), not improvised at build time. A slice with too-few grounded minutes is a horror-cadence failure, not a
  victory.

## 34. Threat ontology — program casualties / Echoes / True Unknowns (frozen taxonomy)

### 34.1 PROGRAM CASUALTIES (human / human-adjacent; physically real)
Sources: failed experiments · damaged subjects · conditioned personnel · cybernetic casualties · abandoned patients.
- Core principle (verbatim): **"Someone did this to a human being."** Provenance and pity matter, not spectacle.
- They obey PHYSICAL rules (D011 experimental lines §12/§13; BV-SKILL-008 sensing machinery). No unique superhero
  ability per casualty; deviations trace to their experimental program and pass the ability contract (D011 §21).
- They are not a bestiary; each is a person-scenario revealing the program's cost (D011 §22 human-first ability rule).

### 34.2 ECHOES
An Echo may appear as: person · voice · movement · repeated event · radio communication · environmental reenactment.
- They may: ignore the Hand · react to him · repeat · change slightly · provide information · mislead unintentionally ·
  leave ambiguous evidence.
- **Do NOT determine in D015 whether every Echo shares one mechanism** — the taxonomy stays plural and unresolved
  (D011 §19 ECHO; D012 §19-§24). Echoes are rare events, authored with IDs and scarcity budget, never ambient
  churn; an "echo of the team heard over the ridge" is a budgeted event, not a procedurally generated ghost patrol.

### 34.3 TRUE UNKNOWNS (the closest thing to "demons")
- EXTREMELY rare. Prefer **1–3 recurring/major Unknown identities in Saga 1**, not a bestiary. They are never routine
  combat mobs.
- Possible functional archetypes (NOT frozen as final designs — archetypes only):
  - a DISTANT OBSERVER (watches; does not hunt every time)
  - a WATER/DEPTH-ASSOCIATED PRESENCE (belongs to the Sound; see §36)
  - a COMMUNICATION/VOICE-ASSOCIATED PHENOMENON (radio/intercom/static; see §37)
- Do not lock final visual designs unless already justified; name nothing definitively this directive. Each TRUE
  UNKNOWN introduced later passes the ability contract and the D011 scarcity budget.
